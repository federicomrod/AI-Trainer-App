# Event knowledge: HYROX

Editable reference the coach is given when an athlete's goal mentions
HYROX. Facts below were checked against public sources on 2026-11-16
(links at the bottom). Anything marked UNVERIFIED must be said as
uncertain, never as fact. Update this file when the official rules
change.

## Format

Eight rounds, each a 1 km run followed by one station, in this fixed
order. Total running is 8 km.

1. SkiErg, 1,000 m
2. Sled push, 50 m
3. Sled pull, 50 m
4. Burpee broad jumps, 80 m
5. Row, 1,000 m
6. Farmers carry, 200 m
7. Sandbag lunges, 100 m
8. Wall balls, reps by division

## Loads by division (individual)

| Station | Open women | Open men | Pro men |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sled push (incl. sled) | 102 kg | 152 kg | 202 kg |
| Sled pull (incl. sled) | 78 kg | 103 kg | 153 kg |
| Farmers carry | 2 x 16 kg | 2 x 24 kg | 2 x 32 kg |
| Sandbag lunges | 10 kg | 20 kg | 30 kg |

## Benchmarks

- Open median finish, from a promotional analysis of about 700,000
  results: men 1:27:56, women 1:36:20.
- Percentile times (top 10%, top 25% and so on): UNVERIFIED. The sources
  that list them could not be read automatically. If the athlete's goal
  is a percentile, say so, give the median as the only checked anchor,
  and ask them to paste the official benchmark for their age group and
  division. Do not invent a time.

## What it demands (coaching knowledge, not sourced)

- It is running with interruptions: heavy, fatiguing stations sit
  between 1 km runs, so the key skill is running well *after* sled
  work, lunges and carries ("compromised running").
- Aerobic base matters most for most athletes; then sled push/pull,
  lunges and wall balls are where strength-trained athletes lose or gain
  time. Grip and leg endurance limit the carries and lunges.
- Station-specific practice matters in the last 8-10 weeks; before that,
  run volume and general strength work.
